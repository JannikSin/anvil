# 0014. Block 1 settled: about 16 sets on the priority muscles, lettered supersets, no repeated lift

- **Status:** accepted; supersedes the set counts in decision 0013
- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Deciders:** David ("try to get around 16, because let's get more growth"; "once this is all perfectly settled ... it's set in stone")
- **Tenet invoked:** 1 (the plan covers the goal), 2 (evidence), 3 (technique before time)

## Context

Decision 0013 set the range at 10 to 20 weekly sets per muscle and cut to the low-middle of it.
David chose the upper-middle for growth, asked what the superset arrow meant, and asked whether
incline and flat pressing train the same muscle twice in one session.

## Options considered

- **Stay at 0013's counts (chest 12, back 15, quads 15).** Shortest sessions; less growth per week.
- **Push every priority muscle to 20.** Most growth on paper; sessions past an hour and per-session
  sets past the useful ceiling.
- **About 16 on chest, back and quads, side delts about 11 counting pressing, arms 6 to 8 direct.**
  Sessions stay about 40 to 50 minutes; no muscle passes 9 hard sets in one session.

## Decision

The third. Weekly direct sets: chest 14, back 16, quads 16, hamstrings 10, side delts 10 (about
11.5 with overhead pressing), rear delts 4, triceps 8, biceps 6 (each about 6 more from presses and
rows), calves 6, abs 14. Incline (Push A) and flat (Push B) stay on different days: incline biases
the upper chest (Chaves 2020), and together they are 6 of the week's 14 chest sets. Legs A drops its
leg press, a third quad press after the squat and the split squat. Supersets are shown as lettered
pairs (A1 and A2) with the rule in words; uneven pairs say which lift does its extra sets alone.

## Consequences

Each muscle sits in the efficient part of the dose-response curve and every session has one job
per lift. What gets worse: sessions run about 3 minutes longer than under 0013, and arms rely on
indirect work from pressing and rowing to reach the range.
